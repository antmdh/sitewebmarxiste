## A mobile strategy and infiltration game

In the Sing Sing Valley transposes the French war in Indochina into a science-fiction universe. You play as a Venusian officer sent to Rhea, one of Jupiter’s moons, amid a colonial war as unstable as it is unpredictable.

## War as a labyrinth

Every campaign redistributes territories and changes how the player can travel. A Venusian-controlled zone cannot be crossed in the same way as a rebel-held sector: you must read the map, choose a route and adapt to a shifting situation.

Missions combine objectives imposed by the command — securing a depot or interrogating an informant — with personal goals such as finding a missing comrade. Battles can erupt in a harbour, an airport or deep in the jungle.

## A critique of the colonial machine

The game examines the mechanisms of imperialism: civilian bombing, abuses and propaganda are not mere scenery. The player character is not cast as a hero standing outside the conflict, but as a participant whose decisions expose the system’s contradictions.

## Humanity against the machine

Both sides are controlled by algorithms. Combined with the reshuffling of the maps, they make every campaign different and support a reflection on warfare, obedience and the limited space left to individual choice.

## A politically engaged artistic experience

The art direction draws on 1950s and 1960s war cinema, using saturated colours and a science-fiction jungle. Its fragmented narrative gradually reveals military orders and their consequences.

> “War is a labyrinth whose map belongs to no one.”
